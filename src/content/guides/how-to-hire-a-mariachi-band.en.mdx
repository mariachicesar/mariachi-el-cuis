# How to hire a mariachi band in Los Angeles

The best mariachi for your event is not simply the first group with an open date. Before paying a
deposit, make sure the quote describes the same group, schedule, and service you expect. This
checklist makes it easier to compare mariachi bands in Los Angeles on equal terms.

## Start with the details of your event

Send every band the same basic information so their quotes are comparable:

- Event date and exact start time
- Venue address or city
- Occasion, such as a wedding, birthday, quinceañera, funeral, or serenade
- Requested performance length
- Indoor or outdoor setting
- Any ceremony cues, announcements, or must-play songs

An address matters because travel distance can affect availability, minimum hours, and price. If
the venue has a loading procedure, gate, parking fee, or stairs, mention that before booking too.

## Ask what the quote includes

A useful quote should tell you the performance length, total price, deposit, remaining balance, and
cancellation terms. Ask whether travel, parking, and special song preparation are included. Also
confirm whether breaks are part of a multi-hour booking and how much actual playing time to expect.

Do not compare one band's hourly price with another band's package price without checking the
details. Our [Los Angeles mariachi cost guide](/en/guides/mariachi-cost-los-angeles) explains our
weekday package, hourly rates, distance minimums, deposit, and cancellation policy.

## Verify the group before paying

Recent, full-length performance video is more useful than a short edited clip. Listen for balanced
vocals, clean entrances, and the sound you want for your event. Confirm that the contact taking the
deposit represents the group you expect to arrive.

Get these details in writing:

- Band name and direct contact information
- Date, arrival time, start time, and end time
- Venue address
- Agreed total, deposit, and balance
- Cancellation or rescheduling policy
- Special songs and event cues

Keep the confirmation and payment receipt. A clear written agreement protects both the client and
the musicians from last-minute misunderstandings.

## Choose enough time for the occasion

A short serenade and a wedding reception need different plans. Think in moments, not just hours:
which entrance, toast, dance, or dedication should the mariachi cover? Leave a little room in the
schedule for guests to gather before an important song.

For weddings, use our [mariachi wedding timeline](/en/guides/wedding-mariachi-timeline). For a
quinceañera, see the [quinceañera song guide](/en/guides/quinceanera-song-guide).

## Plan the song requests

Ask whether your essential songs are already in the band's repertoire and send unusual requests
early. A title and the singer's name or a recording link can prevent confusion between songs with
similar names. Browse our [mariachi repertoire](/en/repertoire) and our [song ideas by
occasion](/en/guides/best-mariachi-songs-by-occasion) before making your list.

## Before the band arrives

Give the musicians one on-site contact who can answer the phone, show them where to enter, and cue
any surprise. Confirm where they can park and where they should wait without being seen. For an
outdoor event, plan shade or cover and check the venue's sound rules.
