# Mariachi songs for funerals and burials

Mariachi music can honor a loved one with warmth, dignity, and a connection to family history. The
right funeral songs are usually the songs that person loved, not only songs traditionally associated
with loss. Start with their favorites, then use this guide to fill the remaining moments.

## Songs for saying goodbye

- **Amor Eterno** is a deeply emotional choice for remembrance and farewell.
- **Las Golondrinas** traditionally marks a departure and often closes a service.
- **Te Vas Ángel Mío** expresses the pain of losing someone dear.
- **Cruz de Olvido** carries a reflective, solemn ranchera feeling.
- **Un Mundo Raro** can suit a personal tribute centered on enduring love.

Lyrics can feel different after a loss. Read them in full and choose the version that reflects the
person and the family. The funeral director or church may also have rules about when secular music
can be played.

## Songs to honor a mother or father

For a parent, a song connected to family memories may be more meaningful than a conventional
funeral selection. **Amor Eterno** and **Hermoso Cariño** are frequent starting points for a mother,
father, or grandparent. **Paloma Querida** and **Gema** can honor a spouse or a person remembered
with great affection.

If your loved one always requested **El Rey**, **Volver, Volver**, or **México Lindo y Querido** at
celebrations, including that favorite can turn the tribute from a generic service into a portrait of
their life.

## Music for a Catholic funeral Mass

Music during Mass must follow the parish's liturgical guidelines. Ask the priest or music director
which parts may include mariachi and whether every selection must be sacred. Do this before
finalizing the list with the band.

Possible musical moments include:

- Gathering or entrance
- Presentation of gifts
- Communion
- Final commendation
- Recessional outside the church

The family, church, and mariachi should agree on the order and cues in advance. An approved hymn or
instrumental selection may be required inside, while a personal ranchera may fit better outside.

## Viewing, service, and graveside planning

A mariachi may be part of the viewing, funeral or memorial service, graveside committal, or a family
gathering afterward. You do not need music at every stage. Choose the moments where live music will
support the family rather than interrupt speakers, prayers, or military honors.

For a graveside service, confirm cemetery rules, parking, walking distance, start time, and where the
musicians should stand. Outdoor conditions and a delayed procession can affect the schedule, so give
the band a funeral-home or family contact for day-of coordination.

Some cemeteries limit graveside music. Resurrection Cemetery in Montebello, for example, allows only
three to seven songs. Most cemeteries have no limit, but confirm before you finalize the list.

## A simple song order

For a brief tribute, three to five songs can create a complete arc:

1. A welcoming song associated with the person's life
2. A family dedication
3. A quiet song for reflection
4. Their favorite mariachi song
5. **Las Golondrinas** as the farewell
